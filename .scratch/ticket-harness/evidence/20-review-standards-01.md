## Substantive assessment

The source meets the ticket's ownership and repository design standards within the inspected scope. The decisive improvement is that one attempt operation now owns the coordinated historical/current-ticket writes. This conclusion follows from source and caller tracing, separately from the passing checks below.

`tickets._Attempt` (`tickets.py:24–104`) owns the canonical attempt dictionary and the existing current-ticket projection. Construction appends the same dictionary that later operations modify, computes the inclusive gate start from the canonical gate sequence, and establishes Running. Submission changes state, submitted revision and observed changed files together and projects the existing fields onto the ticket. Candidate observation updates both locations; completion adds Done, the promoted revision and the exclusive gate end. Rejection records its diagnostic and exclusive range even when source preparation, report validation or capture failed before a submitted revision existed.

The projections intentionally differ. Rejected is an attempt observation; the ticket retains its last Running/Verifying observation until the next attempt begins or the run classifies terminal failure as Blocked. Failure and gate end stay in attempt history. Source is initially attempt-only and becomes part of the ticket through the existing submission projection. `complete` also preserves the prior ticket shape rather than copying the historical gate end. Comparing these operations with the fixed baseline establishes preservation of existing keys and intermediate values, including failures before submission.

All migrated writers use these operations. `tickets.execute` constructs a fresh owner per initial/repair assignment and supplies that precise instance through its bound `check` caller. It no longer initializes or updates the lifecycle dictionary itself. The owner receives the concrete ticket/history/gate observations and identities, with no runner/configuration/Git callback dependency. The runner no longer writes an attempt or chooses one by history position. `attach_human_decision` checks ticket, attempt and assigned revision before updating both existing handoff locations. The constructor, submit, reject and complete operations encapsulate the coupled rules; this is not a getter/setter-only wrapper. Removing it would scatter those coordinated writes back across execution and checking.

The unchanged execution loop still owns bounded repair policy, original approved base, source carrying, repair assignment and revision history. `repair_start` returns the rejected work's observations without assigning a repair or deciding its allowance. Its returned failure dictionary is read and serialized into the next assignment, with no consumer mutation. The runner's outer Blocked/outcome classification and Pending/Ready dependency calculations remain separate from attempt lifecycle. Their unchanged writes are appropriate to this slice; demanding their migration would consume later refactors.

Canonical checking remains runner-owned. `agents.report` (`agents.py:189–218`) receives stdout and expected identity and creates a fresh local observation. Decoding, duplicate/nonfinite handling, depth bounds, exact identity types and complete implementation/review validation remain in place. Parsed content is retained only after decoding and depth validation as before; other rejected output remains available through raw stdout. No canonical gate is passed to this parser. `_Run.check` publishes returned content/status/diagnostics, saves an invalid result, then raises its existing report-rejection diagnostic. It never merges untrusted top-level report fields into gate authority: the report is nested in the parser-owned observation.

Failure ordering is preserved. Execution environment errors stop before evaluation; timed-out reports are not evaluated; malformed reports cannot attach decisions. A fully validated implementation decision or blocking decision from either review axis is attached to the explicit owner and makes the failure nonrepairable, including nonzero exits. Complete ordinary success reports do not overcome process failure, and exit-zero blocking findings fail while optional findings remain nonblocking. Integrity retains nonrepairable treatment. Source validation can invalidate canonical evidence and replace the diagnostic at the same existing point. No subsequent gate or extra repair follows a validated decision. Baseline and milestone checks supply neither a decision-producing prompt nor an attempt owner; every ticket check receives its current owner.

Reference aliases and accepted values do not undermine these guarantees. Current-ticket identity is established by `record['ticket'] = record['tickets'][ticket]`; owner construction mutates that existing mapping instead of replacing it, preserving both views. History holds the owner's exact dictionary. Gate ranges refer to the growing canonical list, not a copied list. Git-derived changed files and assignment/report data are only read by their consumers after attachment. Arbitrary extra report metadata stays descriptive, and exact identity validation rejects boolean attempts and mismatches before attachment. Canonical string ticket identities, including leading zeros and multi-ticket ordering, are passed through unchanged. I found no narrower unsupported scalar/input assumption needed for this conclusion.

Persistence remains compatible. `records.write_json` atomically replaces the saved record; saves still expose Running before worktree execution, observed source after the protected operation, Verifying after capture, candidate before its suite, and Done only after expected-old promotion. Rejection saves the historical failure/range before repair or terminal classification. `_Run.check` additionally saves the rejected canonical report before unwinding. No owner object enters JSON. QA's version-1 checkpoint stores existing dictionaries after completed assignments, and future work constructs fresh owners. `restore_checks` reconnects verified entries to `record['gates']`; subsequent invalidation modifies those canonical gates. The unchanged completion-input consumer and status CLI continue reading their documented record shapes. Protection of caller work, approved inputs and historical artifacts remains in the existing Git/process contexts.

The design is proportionate under the OOP, codebase-design and smell guidance. A small operation interface hides coupled observations; pure parsing, readiness calculations, Git primitives and atomic file/process operations remain functions. There is no hierarchy, speculative registry, general state-machine framework or forwarding-method bag. The long execution loop has a cohesive policy responsibility still assigned to ticket execution, and the attempt owner takes responsibility only for its own lifecycle observations. No concrete documented-standard violation or evidence-based optional design suggestion was found. Tool-enforced formatting/type/lint matters are not restated as review findings.
